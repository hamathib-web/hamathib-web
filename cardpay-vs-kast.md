Africa is quietly becoming the world's most active stablecoin economy. According to YouGov and BVNK research, 79% of crypto users in Nigeria and South Africa already hold stablecoins, and 71% said they'd use a stablecoin-linked debit card if given the chance.

The demand is undeniable. What's been missing is **choice**.

For the past two years, Solana's KAST card has been the go-to option for crypto holders looking to spend their digital dollars in the real world. It's a solid product. But "solid" isn't the same as "enough" — especially for a continent of 1.4 billion people with wildly diverse financial needs.

Enter **CardsPay** — a virtual card platform built for the realities of African digital commerce.

## The African Context: Why One Card Isn't Enough

Let's talk about what "spending crypto" actually looks like in Africa:

- **Freelancers** in Kenya and Nigeria receiving USDC from international clients need to pay for tools like ChatGPT, Adobe, and AWS — services that often reject African-issued cards
- **E-commerce sellers** managing ads across Facebook, Google, and TikTok need reliable card access with spending controls
- **Small businesses** paying remote teams across multiple countries need cards that work across currencies
- **Students and young professionals** buying subscriptions for learning platforms, streaming services, and cloud storage

KAST works for some of these use cases. But it wasn't designed with all of them in mind. CardsPay's focus on **practical digital commerce** — not just holding and spending crypto — makes it a better fit for many African users.
